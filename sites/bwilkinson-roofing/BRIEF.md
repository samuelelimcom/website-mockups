# B.Wilkinson Roofing Ltd: concept brief

**Business:** B.Wilkinson Roofing Ltd, Aspull, Wigan. Google rating 5.0 from 56 reviews (prospect
sheet). Phone 01942 909095. No website found by name or phone search (2026-11-27). Public sources
opened in search: the Wigan Council business directory lists the company with roofing, leadwork
and chimney repairs, Aspull address and this phone number; Companies House lists B. WILKINSON
ROOFING LIMITED (14179637), a private limited company. Nothing else (prices, years, insurance,
guarantees, staff, photos) is used, so the mockup describes the trade in general and the footer
says service details are examples to confirm with the owner.

## Who buys this, in Aspull and around Wigan
- **Homeowners in older terraces and semis, 35 to 70.** Stone and red-brick terraces with slate
  roofs and a chimney stack on every house. The trigger is almost always weather: a damp patch on
  the bedroom ceiling after a wet night, a slate on the pavement after a gale, a drip by the
  chimney breast. Fear: a stranger on the roof "finding" more work, being told they need a whole
  new roof when they need three slates, or paying and the leak coming back.
- **Older owners, 65+,** who have lived in the house for decades. Trigger: a neighbour or family
  member points at the chimney or a slipped slate. Fear: cowboys, door-knockers, pressure.
  They want a local name they can ring and a clear explanation.
- **Landlords and buyers.** Trigger: a survey that flags the chimney or flashing, or a tenant's
  leak report. They want a quick, written, plain quote.

## How they arrive and what they need in 10 seconds
On a phone, from Google Maps, often the morning after rain, sometimes standing in the street
looking up at the roof. In ten seconds: *a real local roofer in Aspull, does slates, lead and
chimneys, rated 5.0 by 56 people, I can ring now or send the problem in a minute.* So: name,
trades and rating in the first screen; a thumb-reach Call / Free quote dock; a form whose first
question is "what's the problem?", not "what service do you want?".

## Three competitor types (search, 2026-11-27)
1. **Directory-only Wigan roofers (Checkatrade, MyJobQuote, FreeIndex).** Good: reviews visible,
   real local names. Tired: every firm looks identical; a phone number and a list of keywords,
   nothing that explains the work.
2. **Template roofing sites from larger Greater Manchester firms.** Good: service lists, trust
   badges. Tired: stock photos of American shingle roofs, "No job too small!", walls of
   keywords, pop-ups.
3. **Lead marketplaces.** Good: easy to ask. Tired: the homeowner never knows who turns up.
**The gap:** a site that *shows* how a roof keeps water out (slate courses, lead flashing,
pointing) so the owner understands what they are paying for, from a named local firm.

## Positioning, tone, story
- **Positioning line:** "Keeping Wigan weather on the outside."
- **Tone:** straight-talking Northern tradesman. Short, plain sentences. Explains, never sells
  scare stories, never promises things we cannot verify.
- **Home page arc (scroll story over the 3D roof):**
  1. *Problem:* rain finds the gap. Slipped slates on a wet terrace roof, lifted lead at the
     chimney, crumbling joints in the stack.
  2. *Turn:* the slates go back. Slipped slates slide up into their course.
  3. *Lead and chimney:* the flashing is dressed down tight and the stack is repointed.
  4. *Result:* the rain stops, the sky clears, the roof is dry.
  Then proof (5.0 from 56 Google reviews, link), the three services, "what a visit looks like",
  and the form.

## Look and feel
- **Palette:** wet Welsh slate `#1e2830` (the roof every terrace has; dark, calm, serious),
  Accrington red brick `#a33d26` (the local brick; used for actions only), mortar cream
  `#f1ece3` (lime mortar and sandstone sills; warm, readable for older eyes), lead grey
  `#8b949a` (rules and details), clear-sky blue `#cfe0ea` (the "after", used sparingly).
- **Type:** Schibsted Grotesk (variable, one family). Sturdy, newspaper-like grotesque: reads as
  honest and local, very legible at 17px body.
- **Layout:** square corners and hard edges (slate and brick, not soft lawn), sections stacked
  like courses of slate with a lead-grey rule on top of each, a numbered "course" index, large
  tap targets, one primary action per screen.

## The 3D concept
A cutaway of an Aspull terrace roof: red-brick front wall with a sandstone sill and lintel,
fascia and gutter, then the roof built up layer by layer (rafters, membrane, battens) and
covered in individual blue-grey slates in proper courses, with clay ridge tiles and a brick
chimney stack with pots and lead flashing. Rain falls and the slates look wet. As you scroll,
slipped slates slide back into place, the lead apron is dressed down, the stack is repointed and
the sky clears. Service pages: **roof repairs** (move over the slates to lift and inspect them;
slipped slates are re-fixed), **leadwork** (move along the flashing to dress it down),
**chimney repairs** (move over the stack to repoint it).
