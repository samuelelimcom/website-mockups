# Brief: Hale's Tree Service, LLC (Bowling Green, KY), concept mockup

Facts used (sheet + Google Business Profile only): name, Bowling Green KY, phone (270) 782-9252,
Google rating 4.5 from 11 reviews, profile link. Everything else on the site is general to the
tree-care trade and is marked as an example to confirm with the owner. No prices, years, licences,
insurance claims, crew names, photos or quoted reviews.

## Who buys this, in this town
- Homeowners 35 to 70 in Bowling Green and Warren County: established neighbourhoods off
  Nashville Rd, Scottsville Rd, Smallhouse Rd and the older streets near WKU, where big oaks,
  maples and sweetgums now overhang roofs, drives and power drops. Plus landlords and small
  property managers serving the student rental market.
- Triggers: a storm (Bowling Green has had ice storms, straight-line winds and the December 2021
  tornado, and the January 2026 winter storm brought ice and downed trees across South Central
  Kentucky), a limb that has started to crack or lean over the house, a dead tree the HOA or
  neighbour has mentioned, a stump in the way of a new fence or patio, or selling a house.
- Fears: a tree coming through the roof; a crew damaging the lawn, drive or fence; a price that
  changes on the day; "will they actually turn up and haul it all away?"; liability if a
  neighbour's property is hit.

## How they arrive and what they need in 10 seconds
- On a phone, from Google Maps or a search like "tree removal Bowling Green", often standing in
  the yard looking up at the problem tree, sometimes the morning after a storm.
- In 10 seconds: what this company does (removal, trimming, stumps), that it is local to Bowling
  Green, the Google rating, and two ways to act: call now, or send the tree in under a minute.

## Three local competitors (from public listings and their sites)
1. BG Treeman, LLC (4.7 from 83 Google reviews): strong review count and a clear
   "removal, trimming, stump grinding, storm cleanup, land clearing" list. Its web presence leans
   on Google Business posts and directory pages, so the experience is generic and text-heavy.
2. Saye and Sons LLC: lists stump grinding plus stump excavation and covers nearby towns. Clear
   service scope, but directory-style pages with no sense of how the job is actually done.
3. The many templated "tree service Bowling Green" pages (vercel/netlify/pages.dev clones) and
   lead-gen sites (Thumbtack, LawnStarter, HomeAdvisor): fast to find, but anonymous, stock-photo
   heavy and they sell the lead to several companies.
- Gap: nobody shows the work itself, in order, so a nervous homeowner can picture how a big tree
  comes down safely next to their house. Nobody makes the quote feel personal and quick on a phone.

## Positioning, tone and story
- Positioning: "The tree comes down in pieces. Your yard stays in one."
- Tone: calm, plain-spoken Kentucky tradesman. Short sentences, practical, no hype, no fear-selling.
- Home page arc (scroll story tied to the 3D tree):
  1. Problem: the limb over the roof you keep looking at after every storm.
  2. Turn: it does not come down in one go. Limb by limb, lowered, not dropped.
  3. Result: the trunk in rounds, then the stump ground below the grass. Only a patch of chips.
  then proof (Google rating), services, how it works, the quote form.

## Design choices
- Palette: bark-black green `#1b241c` ink (timber, shade, trust); sawdust cream `#f5efe3`
  background (fresh-cut wood, warm on a sunny phone screen); hi-vis safety orange `#f26a1b` for
  calls to action (the colour of chainsaw chaps and helmets: says "crew" and is the most visible
  colour outdoors); heartwood amber `#c98a46` for accents (the inside of a fresh-cut round);
  leaf green `#4f7a3a` for small highlights.
- Type: Bitter (variable slab serif), one family. Slab serifs read as sturdy, workwear and rural
  signage, a clear break from the sans-serif templated competitor pages; Bitter stays legible at
  16 px on phones.
- Layout: growth-ring motif (concentric rings) for numbers and section markers; a timber-dark
  hero band; story cards that sit at the top of the screen on phones so the tree's crown stays
  visible below; services shown as a "job ticket" list with what is included; big call button
  always docked on phones.

## The 3D concept
- A real-time Kentucky yard: a mature hardwood with ridged bark (procedural PBR bark and bump),
  a heavy limb reaching over a shingled roof corner, leafy crown clumps, lawn and soil.
- Scroll on the home page plays the job: the limb is cut and lowered on a rope, the crown comes
  down in sections, the trunk is cut into rounds that land showing heartwood and growth rings,
  then a stump grinder wheel chews the stump down to a patch of wood chips.
- Pointer or touch: drag sideways to walk around the tree (orbit); the scene leans toward the pointer.
- Service pages loop their part of the job: removal, trimming (limb cut and lower), stump
  grinding (stump ground to chips).
