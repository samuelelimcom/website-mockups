# Greater A Lawncare: concept brief

**Business:** Greater A Lawncare, Macon, GA. Google rating 5.0 from 22 reviews. No website found
by name or phone search (2026-11-26). Phone +1 478-229-9127. Everything else about the business
(exact services, service area, prices, crew) is unknown, so the mockup describes lawn care in
general and the footer says service details are examples to confirm with the owner.

## Who buys this in Macon
- **Busy households, 30 to 55.** Two jobs, kids' sports on Saturdays. The trigger is the spring
  green-up (April) when warm-season grass starts growing faster than they can keep up with, or a
  week of rain that leaves the yard a foot tall. Fear: hiring someone who shows up twice and
  disappears, or who scalps the lawn and leaves clippings on the porch.
- **Older homeowners, 60+,** in established neighbourhoods with mature trees. Trigger: the mower
  got too heavy, a fall, a doctor's advice. Fear: being overcharged, strangers in the yard, a
  company that is hard to reach by phone.
- **Landlords, sellers and new arrivals** (Robins AFB moves, Mercer staff). Trigger: a listing
  photo, a code-enforcement notice, an HOA letter, moving day. Fear: the lawn costs them a sale or
  a fine. They want it done once and done right.

## How they arrive and what they need in 10 seconds
Phone, from the Google Maps listing, often standing in the yard looking at the problem. In ten
seconds they need: *this is a real local crew, they mow and edge, other neighbours rate them 5.0,
and I can ask for a price without a phone call.* So: name + what they do + rating in the first
screen, a thumb-reach "Free quote" and "Call" dock, and a form that works one-handed.

## Three competitor types (from search, 2026-11-26)
1. **National franchise local pages (US Lawns of Macon).** Good: clear service tree, commercial
   credibility. Tired: stock photos of strangers, written for property managers, no personality,
   nothing that says "your street".
2. **Lead marketplaces (LawnStarter, GreenPal, Angi, Thumbtack).** Good: instant pricing feel,
   reviews visible, booking is easy. Tired: the homeowner never knows who is coming; the local
   crew is a commodity line in a list.
3. **Small local outfits on directories only (HomeGuide, BBB, Facebook).** Good: real people,
   local phone numbers. Tired: no site at all or a one-page template with a phone number and a
   clip-art mower.
**The gap:** a local crew that feels like a neighbour but makes asking for a quote as easy as a
marketplace, and shows the craft (straight stripes, crisp edges) instead of stock photos.

## Positioning, tone, story
- **Positioning line:** "Come home to a yard that's already handled."
- **Tone:** plain-spoken, warm, a little Southern, short sentences. Confident about the craft,
  never about things we cannot verify (no years, licences, guarantees, prices).
- **Home page arc (scroll story over the 3D lawn):**
  1. *Problem:* middle Georgia grass doesn't wait for your weekend (tall, shaggy, creeping over
     the sidewalk).
  2. *Turn:* the first pass. A mower lays down the first stripe.
  3. *Craft:* the edge. Grass that crept over the concrete is cut back to a clean line.
  4. *Result:* the whole yard striped and edged, seen from above, like coming home.
  Then proof (5.0 / 22 reviews, link to Google), services, how it works, quote form.

## Look and feel
- **Palette:** deep pine green `#18301f` (pine trees everywhere in middle Georgia; trust,
  outdoors), Georgia red-clay terracotta `#b9542e` (the soil every Macon gardener knows; used for
  calls to action), warm cream `#f6f0e2` (paper, porch paint; softer than clinical white for
  older eyes), fresh-cut lime `#b7d84b` (the stripe highlight, used sparingly), straw gold
  `#e7b448` for the concept bar.
- **Type:** Bricolage Grotesque (variable, one family). Friendly, slightly hand-made grotesque:
  feels local and human, still very legible at 17px body for older readers.
- **Layout:** big, calm blocks with lots of cream space; striped section bands that echo a
  mown lawn; cards with soft corners; large tap targets; one primary action per screen.

## The 3D concept
A real cut block of Macon lawn: a slab of turf thousands of blades thick sitting on a visible
cross-section of thatch, dark topsoil and **red Georgia clay** with roots, beside a strip of
concrete sidewalk. A push mower (built from primitives) mows it in rows as you scroll, leaving
light/dark stripes, then the overgrowth on the sidewalk is edged back to a crisp line. On
desktop you can steer the mower with the pointer; on phones you swipe across the lawn to mow it.
Service pages reuse the block: mowing (auto-mow loop), yard cleanup (autumn leaves you can blow
off with your pointer) and pine straw (a bed around a shrub that fills with straw).
